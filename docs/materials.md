# Materials and circularity

Every part should carry or reference a durable material-passport ID containing material grade and additives, reinforcement, supplier/lot, process parameters, mass, recycled-content fraction, machine/operator/date, quality record, repair method, inspection interval, retirement criteria, and end-of-life route.

## Preferred research architecture

- **Frame shells and covers:** recycled/recyclable thermoplastic; use qualified continuous-fiber or metal inserts only where analysis requires them.
- **Axles, bearing seats, and brake interfaces:** replaceable, qualified metal or composite inserts; never hobby-print a rideable axle.
- **Tire:** replaceable cast solid elastomer or validated airless thermoplastic structure, with grip/heat/fatigue testing.
- **Belt:** supplier-manufactured polyurethane synchronous belt with traceable aramid/Kevlar-class cords; no improvised rope or fabric loop.
- **Internal hub:** serviceable bearings, gears, clutch, sensors, and sealed manufacturer-specified lubricant.
- **Motor/controller:** replaceable standard modules and documented connectors.
- **Battery:** qualified removable pack with BMS and recycling route; cells are not a structural frame member.

Prefer reversible fasteners, separable material families, marked inserts, accessible tools, service drawings, and published replacement tolerances. Avoid permanent battery/controller encapsulation.

Track mass, embodied-carbon estimate, recycled content, service life, replaceable-module count, repair time, separable mass fraction, reclaimed-material yield, and measured energy per kilometre. Do not claim sustainability superiority without a declared lifecycle boundary and functional unit.
