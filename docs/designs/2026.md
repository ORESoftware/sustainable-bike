# 2026 Design: Qualification Baseline

The 2026 profile freezes a conservative research baseline so structural, wheel-retention, drivetrain, motor-control, braking, and battery tests can be correlated against one stable geometry.

## Architecture

- One 60-tooth front ring and one 22-tooth rear belt sprocket.
- Dry 12 mm synchronous belt with supplier-qualified aramid/Kevlar-class cords.
- Two fully automatic internal rear ratios: 1.00 and 1.36.
- No rider-operated shifter, derailleur, cassette, exposed chain, or lubrication schedule.
- 26-inch service envelope with solid microcellular tire geometry.
- Single-sided captive front and rear wheel cartridges with no quick release.
- 500 W rated rear-hub pedal assist; 25 mph private-course mode disabled by default.
- One locked, serviceable 480 Wh LFP battery cartridge.

## Program purpose

This is the qualification datum. Test fixtures, strain-gauge locations, axle-retention proof loads, brake thermal tests, belt alignment, motor torque reaction, ingress tests, and battery-system evidence should be referenced to the exact configuration hash before physical work begins.

## Known tradeoffs

The solid tire removes punctures but increases unsprung mass, impact input, rolling loss, and potential heat build-up. The two-speed hub has a useful direct ratio but lacks a true low starting ratio. The idealized 25 mph continuous-power margin is intentionally narrow, so the target is not promised under wind, grade, low state of charge, or thermal derating.
